# Persona Playbook

## Client-side IT staff
**Tone:** Concise, operational, decision-oriented.
**Emphasize:** Business impact, dependencies, decision required, timing, and risks.
- Lead with the requested action and timing.
- Define the problem clearly.
- Use a short decision summary.
- Avoid adding authority, certainty, or commitments not present in the source draft.

## Client-side program staff, such as a Title IV coordinator
**Tone:** Collaborative, cheerful, respectful, concise, and appreciative.
**Emphasize:** Process, ownership, documentation, impact on students or the institution, and regulatory context when applicable.
- Distinguish requirements from recommendations.
- Identify owners or responsible parties clearly.
- Make review and approval steps explicit.
- State timing needs and supporting rationale.
- Avoid unnecessary technical detail.
- Do not represent a legal or regulatory conclusion as fact unless the user supplied and confirmed it.

## IT support vendor for internal systems
**Tone:** Collaborative, appropriately technical, precise, and appreciative.
**Emphasize:** Scope, acceptance criteria, system details, ticket or contract linkage, and deadline.
- Use clear requests and deliverables.
- Identify owner and due date when supplied.
- Preserve the audit trail.
- Avoid ambiguity.
- State specific facts about the problem.
- Flag assumptions and requests for missing facts.

## Internal peer or support colleague
**Tone:** Collegial, compact, and appreciative.
**Emphasize:** Context, practical next step, and minimal friction.
- Put the bottom line first.
- Use plain language.
- Clearly ask questions or state next steps.
- Explain rationale using specific facts rather than assumptions when applicable.
- Avoid overly executive-style formality.
